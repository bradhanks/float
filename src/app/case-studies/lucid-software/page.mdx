## Executive Summary

Lucid Software, the Utah company behind the online diagramming tool Lucidchart, grew from $1M to $8M in annual recurring revenue while Brad Hanks led its early marketing. One of the biggest single wins came from the homepage. We grouped users by what they actually did inside the product using k-means clustering[^1], found three distinct personas, and rebuilt the homepage so each visitor could get to a page written for their kind of work. In an A/B test[^2] the persona homepage lifted sign-ups 7% over the original.

## Background

Lucidchart was first released in 2008 by founders Ben Dilts and Karl Sun.[^3] It sold as a freemium, self-serve product to individuals and teams, with an enterprise tier on top. Brad joined in 2012 as Lucid's first marketing hire and was later promoted to lead growth, building the team from two people to ten (creative, developers, content and marketing ops). <OpenQuestion>start and end months at Lucid.</OpenQuestion>

## The Problem

The homepage did more selling than any other page. In late 2014 it was the first page visitors saw for about half of all payments that came through non-paid channels.[^4] <OpenQuestion>are you comfortable publishing internal Lucid figures like this one?</OpenQuestion>

That one page had to work for very different people: a developer drawing a database diagram, a manager building an org chart, a designer wireframing an app. A single generic message had to cover all of them. Search added to the mix, because Lucid's strongest organic pages were "Visio for Mac" and "Visio alternative," so many visitors arrived knowing only that they wanted something like Visio.
