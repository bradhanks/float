## Executive Summary

ZipBooks, a free accounting app for small businesses based in American Fork, Utah, brought me on as a contractor in July 2015 and as its first hire that September. At the time its users were mostly friends and family. Over the next three years I built the demand engine from scratch: a new name and domain, the website, search rankings, review profiles, Google integrations and lifecycle email[^1]. ZipBooks grew to more than 25,000 users who had sent at least one invoice. In July 2016, a year after I started, it closed a $2M seed round led by Peak Ventures at an $8M valuation.[^2] Divvy acquired ZipBooks in 2019.[^3]

## Background

Tim Chaves started the company in 2015 as Phlo[^3], an online invoicing tool meant to make money from invoice financing[^4], with early backing from Peak Ventures.[^5] Over time it grew into full double-entry[^6] accounting software for small businesses, with monthly premium plans for features like machine-learning expense categorization and bank integration. The core product stayed free and self-serve, so every new account had to come from someone finding ZipBooks and signing up on their own.

I started on contract in July 2015 and became the first full-time hire that September, with a two-year funding runway to work against.

## The Problem

Accounting software is a crowded search market. QuickBooks, FreshBooks and Xero owned the terms small business owners type into Google, and they charged monthly for features ZipBooks gave away. A free product only grows if people can find it, and ZipBooks had little to be found by:

- A product name, Phlo, that said nothing about accounting.
- No domain authority[^7], no reviews and no press.
- A user base made up mostly of friends and family.

## The Approach

### Name and domain

The team wanted a name that said what the product did. Zipbooks.com had been parked for about 10 years. In August 2015 I traded web work for it: I moved two of the owner's sites onto WordPress with new themes and cloned a third, and he signed over the domain and its traffic. Phlo users got the "Hello, ZipBooks" email on September 9, 2015.

### Website and search

I built the new site in WordPress from simple Adobe design files and coded the theme myself. Then I wrote landing pages around the terms buyers searched, such as "billing software" and "free accounting software." ZipBooks reached the top 3 on Google for "free accounting software," and that ranking brought in most of the signups. An old parked domain comes with junk links, so I wrote guest posts for marketing blogs like Duct Tape Marketing to earn clean editorial links.

### Reviews and launch

Small business buyers compare tools on G2 Crowd and Capterra before they sign up. In spring 2016 I asked early users to review ZipBooks on G2, and on May 10, 2016 we launched on Product Hunt. By mid-2017 ZipBooks had 52 G2 reviews averaging 4.8 out of 5.

### Integrations and sales tools

Owners already worked in Gmail and Chrome all day, so I built the Google Workspace (then G Suite) and Chrome Web Store integrations. I also worked on the Spiceworks app listing and set up HubSpot CRM for an intern salesperson.

### Lifecycle email

Through Intercom, new users got a welcome email, a check-in asking whether they loved ZipBooks yet, and a survey asking what they hated about it. Asking for complaints in the first week surfaced problems while there was still time to fix them, before those users quietly left.

### A second revenue line

I also launched and grew an online bookkeeping service, which ZipBooks later sold to extend its runway. That story gets its own case study.

## Results

- More than 25,000 users who signed up and sent at least one invoice.
- $2M seed round led by Peak Ventures at an $8M valuation in July 2016, a year after I started.[^2]
- Top 3 on Google for "free accounting software," the source of most signups.
- 4.8 out of 5 on G2 across 52 reviews by mid-2017.
- Acquired by Divvy in 2019. Bill.com later bought Divvy.[^3]

## Why it worked

A free product with no sales team lives or dies on being found. Every page that ranked, every review and every marketplace listing did a salesperson's job around the clock, and each one made the next easier to win. That is how a friends-and-family user base grew past 25,000.
